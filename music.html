Project 01 · DAFx 2026 Demonstrations · Accepted

Evaluating tokenization strategies for expressive classical piano performance generation

A controlled study of how symbolic representations encode timing, velocity, beat structure, and sustain pedal for conditional piano generation.

The model uses a condition-aware Transformer decoder to generate MIDI tokens from composer and genre context. Six tokenization modes separate representational choices from architecture, and a two-stage MAESTRO-to-ASAP training procedure evaluates their effect on expressive generation.

Project 02 · Lyrekos · Ongoing

Networked music performance

An online performance environment designed to help geographically separated musicians rehearse and perform together.

My work considers how a system can follow a live performer, align musical positions, and adapt accompaniment timing through real-time audio stretching. The goal is to preserve musical coordination under changing tempo and network conditions.

  • Online performance
  • Audio alignment
  • Time stretching
  • Real-time systems

This description intentionally stays at the non-confidential research level. Company-specific algorithms, system parameters, and implementation details are not disclosed.

Research scope

Networked Music Performance (NMP) combines real-time audio systems with musical structure: the system must estimate where a performer is, determine how the accompaniment should respond, and make timing corrections without disrupting musical continuity.

The project connects music information retrieval with practical performance tooling rather than treating synchronization as a purely technical latency problem.

Project 03 · Ongoing research

Physical sound synthesis & cross-modal retrieval

Learning compact representations of resonant objects, then connecting physical structure to perceived sound.

The synthesis component uses a neural network to predict an eigenspace representation and a Rayleigh–Ritz (RR) solver to recover physically meaningful modes. A complementary contrastive-learning objective aligns material, geometry, impact position, and audio in a shared embedding space.

  • Physical modeling
  • Neural eigenspaces
  • Rayleigh–Ritz
  • Contrastive learning
  • Cross-modal retrieval

Two connected questions

Synthesis: Can a learned eigenspace make physical sound generation efficient while preserving modal structure?

Retrieval: Can a shared representation retrieve sounds from material and shape—or infer physical properties from a recorded impact?

The combined project treats sound as evidence of an object’s material, geometry, and excitation rather than as an isolated waveform.
