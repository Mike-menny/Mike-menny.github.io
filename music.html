AMIDI: Audio-Supervised Event Modeling for Expressive MIDI Generation

Professional musicians need editable MIDI that retains note-level control over performance expression.

Current symbolic music models face two limitations: high-quality expressive MIDI-audio pairs are scarce, and many training targets omit explicit performance controls such as velocity, CC1 dynamics, and sustain pedal. The resulting output may be editable, but it often lacks the continuous, human-like expression retained in audio.

AMIDI uses original audio as an additional supervision signal during pretraining. A shared causal MIDI language model learns expressive event sequences while an auxiliary audio objective encourages its hidden states to capture dynamics present in audio. The model is then fine-tuned on higher-quality paired MIDI-audio data, producing editable MIDI with notes, velocity, CC1, and pedal events.

Evaluating Tokenization Strategies for Expressive Classical Piano Performance Generation

A systematic evaluation of six symbolic representations that add velocity, beat annotations, and sustain pedal to a note-only representation.

The model is a decoder-only Transformer with 24 layers. Composer and genre embeddings condition each layer through cross-attention. Training uses MAESTRO pretraining followed by ASAP fine-tuning.

Physical Sound Synthesis and Cross-Modal Retrieval
